## Design Context

### Users

The primary audience is people working in adjacent fields: AI founders, product builders, engineers, and employees at technology companies. Other readers should still be able to understand the site without insider knowledge.

Visitors come to quickly understand who Tianxin Shi (SToneX) is, what AI products he is currently building, and what he has been thinking and writing about—especially AI agents and related technologies.

### Brand Personality

Thoughtful, restrained, and forward-looking. The site should feel quietly confident and intellectually active: someone with many ideas who is also building real products. It should create curiosity and credibility without sounding self-important or promotional.

### Aesthetic Direction

Refined editorial minimalism with a strong personal point of view. The design should feel premium through typography, proportion, rhythm, and precise details rather than decorative effects. Personal interests and experimental character may appear as subtle signatures, but the homepage should not inherit the visual busyness of the archived retro desktop.

Reference qualities include the editorial care and expressive restraint of Interface Craft, the direct biographical clarity of Josh Puckett's personal site, the singular spatial/interaction gesture of Rauno Freiberg's site, the quiet information architecture and personal texture of Paco Coursey's site, and the durable personal-knowledge architecture of Steph Ango's site. These references should inform principles—not layouts, typography combinations, or signature effects—and the design must not closely imitate any single website.

Avoid generic developer-portfolio conventions, startup landing-page tropes, excessive cards, gradients, glassmorphism, neon AI aesthetics, and ornamental motion.

The existing light/dark support and bilingual English/Chinese structure should remain first-class considerations.

### Design Principles

1. Explain the present first: a visitor should understand within seconds that SToneX is building AI products and exploring AI agent technology.
2. Make thinking visible: recent writing and ideas are primary evidence of expertise, not a secondary blog link.
3. Build recognition through one distinctive signature: use a memorable typographic, editorial, or interaction detail rather than many decorative elements.
4. Create luxury through restraint: prioritize typography, whitespace, alignment, and material-like color over visual effects.
5. Sound like a person, not a personal brand: use specific, direct language and avoid inflated positioning statements.
